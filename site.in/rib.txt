<h2>Rib + first row</h2>
<p>Replacement assembly plan (the internal hub-and-strut frame is dropped —
it was too hard to assemble and align). The globe is built as two
hemispheres, assembled independently, then joined at the equator.</p>
<p>Each hemisphere starts from a <strong>reinforcing rib</strong> — a full
ring around the entire diameter, sitting just above the equator (north) or
just below it (south) — whose outer face makes full contact with the inner
(flat-bottom) faces of that hemisphere's first row of tiles. The rib fixes
the first row's position all the way around the seam, so there is no
cumulative error: every subsequent row stacks up from a ring that is already
true.</p>
<p>The rib is not a smooth cylinder on the outside: its outer surface is a
series of flat facets, one per first-row tile, each lying in that tile's
bottom plane, so every facet mates flush with the tile's inner face. Each
facet also carries a centred <strong>stud fin</strong> (3 mm wide, 2 mm deep,
chamfered ends) that seats into a matching socket cut in the tile's
underside, so the first row cannot slide sideways on the rib. The
rib's inside diameter is 20 mm less than the globe's sea-level diameter
(200 mm), i.e. 180 mm (inner radius 90 mm), and it is 10 mm thick. One rib
is printed per hemisphere:
<a href="rib-north.stl" download>rib-north.stl</a> and
<a href="rib-south.stl" download>rib-south.stl</a> (both print flat, bottom
at z = 0).</p>
<p>Here the two ribs and both first rows are shown together (tiles are
semi-transparent so you can see the ribs pressing against their inner faces):
the red rib + yellow tiles are the northern hemisphere's start, the blue rib
+ teal tiles the southern hemisphere's. The thin grey ring marks the equator.
Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom; the rotation axis is the Earth's polar axis.
The faint wireframe sphere is the globe's outer surface (R = 100 mm).</p>
